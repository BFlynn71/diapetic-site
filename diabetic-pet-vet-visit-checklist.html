What to bring to a diabetic pet vet appointment

By the developer of DiaPetic, who cares for Obi, a diabetic Sheepadoodle · Updated November 22, 2026

Rechecks are where your vet decides what comes next. They go better when you arrive with clear records instead of trying to remember the past month on the spot.

On this page
  1. The week before
  2. Checklist
  3. Why a summary helps
  4. After the visit

The week before

  • Ask whether your pet should eat and get insulin as usual on the morning of the visit. Vets have different instructions.
  • Ask whether to bring a urine sample or do a glucose curve at home first.
  • Start a list of questions as they come to you.

Checklist

  • A summary of care since the last visit: shots, meals, glucose readings, weight and anything unusual
  • Your pet's insulin bottle and a syringe, so your vet can check the type and how you give the shot
  • The name of the food and how much is fed at each meal
  • A list of treats, supplements and other medications
  • Recent glucose readings or a home glucose curve
  • Home ketone test results, if you test for ketones
  • Notes on changes in water drinking, urination, appetite or energy
  • Your questions
  • Supplies you need refilled

Why a summary helps

A summary turns weeks of days into something your vet can read in a minute. Patterns stand out that are easy to miss day to day, such as appetite slowly dropping or readings drifting higher.

After the visit

  • Write down any new instructions exactly as your vet gives them.
  • Record lab results, such as a fructosamine test, so they appear in the next report.
  • Add the next appointment to your calendar.
