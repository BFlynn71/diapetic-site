Managing twice-daily pet insulin shots and meal schedules

By the developer of DiaPetic, who cares for Obi, a diabetic Sheepadoodle · Updated November 18, 2026

Many diabetic dogs and cats get insulin twice a day, about 12 hours apart, with meals to match. The routine soon becomes part of the day, but busy mornings, shared care and changes in plans make it easy to lose track. This guide covers ways to keep the schedule steady and the record clear.

Set the routine with your vet

Ask your vet these questions now, before you need the answers:

  • What times should shots be given, and how far apart?
  • Should the shot come before or after the meal?
  • What should I do if my pet does not eat?
  • What should I do if a shot is missed?
  • What should I do if I am not sure whether a shot was given?

Write the answers down where everyone who helps can see them.

How double doses happen

Double doses are rarely about carelessness. Two people share the care, the morning is rushed, and each thinks the other has not given the shot yet. A clear record that everyone checks before giving a shot is the best protection.

Keep one record and log right away

  • Log each shot as soon as it is given, with the real time.
  • Check the record before every shot, every time.
  • Keep the record in one place rather than on scraps of paper.

If you are not sure a shot was given

Do not give another shot to be safe. Call your veterinarian and follow their advice.

Meals

Do not give insulin to a pet that has not eaten unless your vet has told you what to do. Call your vet instead. Record how much of each meal was eaten. A pet that eats less than usual for more than one meal is worth a call to your vet. DiaPetic records each meal as All, Most, Some or None. See Food and nutrition.

Weekends, travel and sitters

Keep shot times steady on weekends and while traveling. When someone else is caring for your pet, give them your vet's written instructions and a contact number. DiaPetic can share a one-page pet information sheet with insulin and vet details. See Pet information sheet.
