When something seems wrong

The red emergency button at the top right of every screen opens Emergency help. It's always OK to call your veterinarian or an emergency clinic.

On this page

Go to an emergency vet now if your pet is:

Collapsed or won't wake up, having a seizure, struggling to breathe, bleeding heavily, or very weak, wobbly or trembling. Use the call and directions buttons for your emergency clinic, or Find nearby.

Help me decide what to do

Answer a few yes-or-no questions (for example whether your pet is weak or wobbly, could have eaten something harmful, isn't eating, is vomiting, or there's a problem with the insulin) and DiaPetic suggests next steps: act now, call your veterinarian today, or call your veterinarian soon. If it's an emergency, don't wait to finish the questions.

Quick guides

Step-by-step guides for: going to an emergency vet now, possible low blood sugar, possible poisoning, needing to be seen today, not eating while on insulin, vomiting or diarrhea, too much insulin, not sure the shot was given, a missed dose, insulin that may be damaged, drinking and peeing more, a high glucose reading, serious and minor injuries, and when something seems off.

Information for the clinic

A summary built from what you've logged: your pet's details and microchip, insulin and dose, the last shot, last meal and last glucose reading, other medications, today's notes and your regular vet. Read it over the phone, or tap Share or Copy.

Poison control

The screen has one-tap calls to the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center, (888) 426-4435, and the Pet Poison Helpline, (855) 764-7661, for anything your pet may have swallowed. A consultation fee may apply.

Signs of low blood sugar include weakness, wobbling, trembling, twitching, confusion and seizures. Rub honey or corn syrup on the gums and call your vet or an emergency clinic right away.
