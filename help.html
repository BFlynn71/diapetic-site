How to use

Getting started with DiaPetic

A short guide from setting up your pet to sharing reports with your veterinarian. New to pet diabetes? Take it one section at a time.

1. Set up your pet

The first time you open DiaPetic, you'll add your pet. Have your veterinarian's instructions and the insulin label nearby.

  • About your pet: name, dog or cat, breed, color, sex, weight and (optionally) the microchip number.
  • Insulin: the insulin your vet prescribed, its strength (U-40 or U-100), and the units and time for the morning and evening shots. DiaPetic records what your vet prescribed. It never suggests a dose.
  • Meals: the food and portions for each meal, and the daily calorie target if your vet gave you one.
  • Your vet and an emergency clinic: names, phone numbers and addresses, so they're one tap away when you need them.

You can change any of this later in the tab named after your pet (the last tab).

2. Your daily routine

The Tracker tab is where your day happens. Morning and evening each get a card with the meal, the shot and any medications.

Logging a shot

  1. Record how much of the meal was eaten: All, Most, Some or None.
  2. Tap Give on the shot. DiaPetic asks when it was given: just now, at the usual time, or a different time.
  3. The card shows the units and the time given, so anyone checking later knows it's done.
Not sure whether a shot was given? Don't give another. Check the Tracker first, and if it isn't logged and no one remembers, call your veterinarian.

The daily check-in

Water, urine, energy, sleep, stool and vomiting all start at Normal and are saved that way. Just change anything that was different today.

Moving between days

Swipe left or right, use the arrows beside the date, or tap the calendar button to jump straight to any day. Tap the date to come back to today.

3. The journal

Under the check-in, the Journal is for anything else worth remembering: how the day went, something new, a question for your vet.

  • Tap Write about today, then tap the microphone and just talk. Your words appear as text. You can also type, or fix words afterward.
  • A day can have several entries, each with its own time.
  • Journal entries appear in History and in your reports, so your vet sees them too.

4. Reminders

Turn reminders on in your pet's tab › Reminders. You can choose:

  • Insulin time, and a nudge if a shot hasn't been logged
  • Meals, medications and glucose checks
  • Running low on insulin, syringes or food, and time to open a new insulin bottle
  • Vet appointments and checkups
  • Your own reminders, such as heartworm or flea and tick (Pro)

When a reminder appears, press and hold it to snooze for 15 minutes or an hour. Tapping it opens the right pet and screen.

If reminders don't show up, check that notifications are allowed for DiaPetic in iPhone Settings, and that a Focus mode isn't silencing them.

5. Insulin, syringes and food

The Supplies section at the bottom of today's Tracker shows what's on hand and when it runs out.

  • Insulin: the bottle in use, how many days it's been open, and spare bottles in the fridge. When you start a new bottle, tap Open new bottle. Add bottles you pick up with Got a new bottle.
  • Syringes: the box in use and unopened boxes. When you start a new box early, choose whether to keep the leftovers so the count stays right.
  • Food: how much is left based on the meals you log.

Prescription details from the label (refills left, Rx number, pharmacy) live in your pet's tab › Insulin bottles.

6. Your veterinarian

The Vet tab keeps appointments, what to bring, and questions you want to ask.

  • Add an appointment with its start and end time. DiaPetic can add it to your iPhone calendar and keep it updated if you change or cancel it.
  • Jot down questions as they come up, then email your vet with a summary attached.
  • Call or get directions with one tap.

7. Reports and sharing

The Reports tab shows how things are going over 7 days to 6 months: shots given and missed, appetite, symptoms, weight, nutrition, glucose and your journal.

  • PDF report for your vet (Pro): in Reports, tap Share a PDF report with your veterinarian, pick a period, and send it by email, Messages or AirDrop, or print it.
  • Info sheet: in your pet's tab, tap Share beside the name for a one-page sheet with your pet's details, insulin routine, meals, vet and emergency contacts. It's handy for pet sitters, boarding and emergency visits. Free.

8. When something seems wrong

The red emergency button at the top right of every screen opens help right away:

  • One-tap calls to your vet, your emergency clinic and pet poison hotlines
  • Nearby emergency vets
  • Step-by-step guides for low blood sugar, too much insulin, an unsure or missed shot, not eating, vomiting and possible poisoning
  • A summary to read to the clinic: insulin, last shot, last meal and today's notes
Signs of low blood sugar include weakness, wobbling, trembling, confusion and seizures. Rub honey or corn syrup on the gums and call your vet or an emergency clinic right away.

9. Backups and a new iPhone

Everything you enter is stored on your iPhone. To keep it safe:

  • Go to your pet's tab › Backup and restore and tap Back up now. Save the file to iCloud Drive or anywhere in Files.
  • DiaPetic also saves an automatic copy every week and keeps the last four.
  • On a new iPhone, install DiaPetic, open Backup and restore, choose Restore from backup and pick your file.

Remember: DiaPetic keeps records and reminds you. It never recommends insulin doses or changes to treatment. Always follow your veterinarian's instructions.
