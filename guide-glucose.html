Glucose, ketones and lab results

Monitor glucose at home only if your veterinarian has asked you to. To turn on home monitoring, go to your pet's tab › Home tests and turn on I check glucose at home.

Setting your thresholds

Set Highlight readings below and Highlight readings above to the values your veterinarian recommends. These thresholds determine how readings are colored, when the low-reading alert appears and how time in range is calculated. You can choose mg/dL or mmol/L in the app settings.

Recording a reading

On the Tracker, tap Add reading in the Blood glucose section. Enter the value and time, and choose the source: glucose meter, CGM sensor or at the vet. You can add a note if anything was unusual. If a reading falls below your low threshold, DiaPetic displays a warning with buttons to call for help, and it can remind you to check on your pet again later.

Glucose curves

To send a curve to your veterinarian, open the day in Reports, Glucose and tap Share this curve. DiaPetic creates a picture with the curve, the readings, the shots and any walks that you can send by text message or email.

Walks logged on the Tracker appear as shaded bands on a single day's curve and when you compare curves, which can help explain a dip.

A glucose curve is a series of readings taken over the course of a day. In Reports, switch to Glucose to view your readings as curves, along with weekly glucose, time in range, the relationship between eating and glucose, and any low readings.

Tap Compare curves to overlay up to three days. Veterinarians look closely at the lowest point of a curve and when it occurs to assess how well the insulin is working.

Ketone tests

Ketones can be an early sign of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a serious complication that needs a veterinarian. Test for ketones when your vet asks you to, and when your pet is not eating, is vomiting or seems weak.

To record a test, open the Glucose & ketones tile on the Tracker and tap Add ketone test. Choose how you tested:

  • Blood meter: enter the reading from a meter made for blood ketones (beta-hydroxybutyrate), in mmol/L.
  • Urine strip: match the strip to the color chart on the bottle, then pick the word printed beside that color: Negative, Trace, Small (+), Moderate (++) or Large (+++).

Any urine result above Negative, and any blood result at or above your call level, opens a screen that asks you to call your veterinarian, with buttons to call your vet or an emergency clinic and a link to the DKA guide. Trace on a urine strip asks you to call today, or right away if your pet is not eating, is vomiting or seems weak. DiaPetic never gives advice about insulin.

The blood call level starts at 0.6 mmol/L. Ask your veterinarian which number they want to hear about, and set it in your pet's tab › Home tests.

Ketone tests appear on the Tracker, in History, as a small test-tube mark on a single day's curve in Reports, Glucose (amber or red when it says to call your veterinarian), with the results written out under the chart, in the PDF and CSV reports, and in the clinic summary on the emergency screen.

Ketone tests are free for everyone. They are a record and a reminder to call, not a diagnosis.

Lab results

Record blood work, such as a fructosamine test, in Reports › Lab results › Add lab result or when adding notes after a vet visit. Enter the test, the result, the unit and the date, along with any comments from your vet. Lab results are included in your reports.
