How to track pet insulin bottles, syringes and prescription food supplies

By the developer of DiaPetic, who cares for Obi, a diabetic Sheepadoodle · Updated November 18, 2026

Running out of insulin on a weekend is stressful. A few simple habits keep supplies ahead of need.

On this page
  1. Insulin bottles
  2. Syringes must match the insulin
  3. Prescription food
  4. Refills and deliveries
  5. Supply checklist

Insulin bottles

  • Write the date on each bottle when you open it.
  • How long a bottle can be used after opening depends on the product. Check the label, or ask your vet or pharmacist.
  • Store and handle insulin the way the label says.
  • Ask your vet whether to keep a spare bottle on hand.

Syringes must match the insulin

Pet insulins come in different strengths, and syringes are marked to match: U-40 syringes for U-40 insulin and U-100 syringes for U-100 insulin. Using the wrong syringe gives the wrong amount. Check the box each time you pick up a refill.

DiaPetic shows a warning when your syringes do not match your insulin. Set U-40 or U-100 in Syringes to turn it on.

Prescription food

Keep track of how much food is left and when to reorder. Changing food can change blood sugar, so talk to your vet before switching brands or amounts.

Refills and deliveries

  • Note how many refills are left on each prescription.
  • Reorder about a week before you run out, to allow for delays.
  • If your pharmacy refills on a schedule, note the next delivery date.

DiaPetic shows how long each insulin bottle and box of syringes will last and when to order more. It counts spare bottles and boxes on hand, tells you when a prescription has no refills left, and with Auto refill on, reminds you on delivery day. See Insulin, syringes and food.

Supply checklist

  • Insulin, with the open date written on the bottle
  • Syringes that match the insulin strength
  • Prescription food, with the reorder date noted
  • Glucose test strips and lancets, if you test at home
  • A sharps container for used syringes
  • Your vet's and the emergency clinic's phone numbers
